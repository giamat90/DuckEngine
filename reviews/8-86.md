# Review 8-86: the sideways drift of a rolling prism follows the order of the two contact points

Task: 8-86 (open point of 8-76). Class 2 (physics solver); this change is documentation only. Outcome: **an investigation with no code change: in the scene of 8-76 (a prism rolling along x, mirror-symmetric about the plane z = 0 by its construction) the sign and the size of the sideways drift and of the cross spin depend on the order in which the solver visits the two points of the edge manifold. Reversing that order flips the sign of the drift in 5 of 5 scenes with nearly the same size (within 7 percent) and the same peak cross spin; solving both points from the same velocity state (a Jacobi step over the points of one manifold) takes the drift from 20 to 210 mm to 0 to 24 mm. That Jacobi probe is not a fix: it turns 48 tests of the physics crate red, so the cure is a design (card 8-91), not a default. The order is shown to set the sign; that it is the only source of the asymmetry is not shown.** Owner go-ahead: "go on with wave 1" (2026-11-26), card 8-86 taken first.

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
  participant S as solver (one manifold, two points A and B)
  participant P as prism (symmetric about z = 0)
  Note over S: Gauss-Seidel: A is solved, its impulse applied, then B is solved on the changed velocity
  S->>P: order A then B: a small yaw impulse of one sign

  P->>P: the drift grows over the hops (8-76: 4 sub-steps 210 mm, 16 sub-steps 4.7 mm; the amplification itself was not tested here)
  Note over S: Jacobi (both from the same state): no preferred side, drift 0 to 24 mm
```

## Decisions

1. No solver change in this card: the mechanism is shown, the probes that remove it break the suite, and a cure moves every 3D golden hash and many pinned tests.
2. The sequential visit of the points of one manifold is the cause named; a card (8-91) is opened for the design: exact block solve of a manifold against a symmetric order, with its cost and its effect on stacks, to be picked by the owner.
3. The default of more relax passes (the lever named in 8-76) is not recommended: it does not remove the order bias (the sign still flips in 5 of 5) and in this probe it worsened 12 sides at 3.0 m/s (71.6 against 20.1 mm; the relax-2 figures here do not agree with 8-76, so this is weak).

## Not verified

- That the point order is the only source of asymmetry: alternating the order does not remove the drift (61 to 187 mm), and the Jacobi probe leaves 24.2 mm and yaw 0.119 for 16 sides at 3.2 m/s (scale 1), 5.3 mm with scale 1/count. The prism corners are exactly mirrored in z, so rounding of the corners is not the reason; the emission order of the points, the tangent basis and the friction cone coupling are not excluded.
- Why the sign is deterministic for a given order, and whether the order of the manifold points is stable from step to step.
- That the sample is small: five scenes chosen in 8-76 because they drift, one run each; no control scene below 2.2 m/s was run here (8-76 gives at most 4.7 mm).
- The probes are not saved: a variable read in `constraint.rs` that selects the order (reversed, or alternating by a counter) or defers the pushes of the points of a manifold until all are solved (Jacobi, delta scaled by 1 or by 1/points), and a test printing the five scenes; the numbers cannot be reproduced from the repository.
- Why the probe breaks 48 tests; which of them are the probe's friction and restitution handling (restitution was left in sequence) and which are Gauss-Seidel tuning.
- Cost of any cure; Linux and ARM64 (Windows, release profile, one run each, 240 steps); other shapes with edge manifolds (boxes on an edge, capsules).
- That the same order bias moves the box and the hull cube scenes (not run); the 2D solver (not read).
- The local gates (card 8-88 ran them on `develop`; this change adds a report). Mermaid read by the author, not rendered.
